The wordmark

MADE in the block, Saint in the blackletter. This is the mark on the house.

What the 9060 teaches

Measured from the lateral view of the New Balance 9060, applied to our locked flat.

Length
Centred at half the shoe length, spanning about 37% to 63%: the mark owns the midfoot, between the heel overlay and the toe.
Top
Tucked against the eyestay, as if the lacing sits on it. No gap that makes it float.
Bottom
Ends on the lower overlay, about a sixth of the side height above the midsole. Never on the foam.
Height
Fills about 80% of the side between eyestay and midsole.
Lean
15° forward, the italic of the letter, not the rake of the panels.
Layering
Sits on top of every panel and crosses the seams, so it reads as one graphic, not a panel filler.
Keyline
A border a step darker than the mark separates it from mesh and suede.
Front
Stops short of the forefoot flex zone.

Placement

Four positions on the locked lateral flat, all tonal. The red band is the forefoot flex zone, where the upper creases on every step.

Midfoot

Locked
  • Placed by the 9060 rules: centred at half length, top tucked under the eyestay, bottom on the lower overlay
  • Crosses the mesh window and the saddle, so it holds the side together
  • Stops before the forefoot flex zone, so the overlay does not stiffen the crease

Quarter sweep

Previous
  • Sat too far back and ran down to the midsole, so it read as a heel graphic
  • The 20° rake fought the letter's own italic

Mesh window

Alternative
  • Contained in the rear mesh window, cheapest to make
  • Too small to carry the brand from across a room

Full length

Rejected
  • Reads as borrowed from the 700 MNVN
  • Runs through the forefoot flex zone, where bonded film cracks

Specification, locked

15° forward

Bottom

Swatch

Colour
Tonal only: mark one step lighter than the overlay, keyline one step darker

Solid molded TPU, about 8 mm wide, three peaks toward the laces
