# MADE SAiNT · MS-TM01 Trail Mule

Prototype 001 construction manual. One handmade pair. US men’s 12 wide (2E). Colorway 04, powder blue / fog grid, with black rubber.

Revision A · 19 October 2026.

The reference card is the design authority. This manual tells a maker how to build one proof pair without inventing a new shoe. Drawings in `drawings/` are proportion studies. They are not measured patterns. Every unverified figure is marked **PROVISIONAL — PHYSICAL VALIDATION REQUIRED**.

This pair is not certified outdoor, trail, or winter footwear. Walk-test it indoors and record what changes for revision 002.

## What the card shows

- A low mule. The heel is open. There is no lace and no vamp tongue.
- A rounded toe sitting behind a black rubber toe bumper.
- A wide black outsole with a sculpted wave sidewall and a field of plus-form lugs. Each lug on the card has a small center pip.
- A powder-blue ripstop upper with a light windowpane grid. The grid runs over the padded collar.
- A rolled, tonal, padded collar. The edge is the same cloth, not a contrast binding.
- A small stitched tab on the instep collar, centered, debossed MADE / SAiNT. The lowercase i is required. Match the founder wordmark file. Do not redraw the letters. It is not a tongue and it is not a label on the backstay.
- A removable powder-blue footbed. Saint Michael is printed in slate, head and wings toward the toe, the figure underfoot toward the heel, and MADE SAiNT beneath the scene.

## What is not on the card

Record these. Do not build them in unless a later sample asks for them.

- Navy elastic side gores are in the 19 October memorandum. They are not visible on the card. Leave them out of prototype 001.
- Navy edge tape is in the memorandum. The card shows a rolled tonal collar. Use the shell cloth for the roll.
- A center front seam and side seams are not on the card. Do not add them for decoration. If the trace needs a close, put a short seam inside the collar roll at the back, not under the instep tab.
- No outsole brand has been selected. Do not describe a commercial sole as specified.
- No last has been selected. A nominal size 12 sole is not assumed to fit a 2E last.

## Materials for one pair

Use `bom/MS-TM01-bom.csv`. Buy allowances there come from the memorandum. They are not cutting yields.

Named listing for the shell: Yardblox A033513, 92% nylon / 8% spandex, 40 GSM, DWR, crinkled ripstop. Swatch it. Confirm the powder blue and whether the white grid is actually in the cloth. A plain listing may not reproduce the photographed grid.

40 GSM is light for a lasted upper. Test a sew-in stabilizer before any fusible touches the DWR face.

## Tools

- US men’s 12 wide (2E) last, once sourced
- The sourced black rubber bottom
- Pattern card, tape, pencil, measure
- Shears or a rotary cutter, clips
- Sewing machine that will take a light stretch woven, ballpoint or stretch needle chosen on a swatch
- Edge-roughing tool, adhesive brush, weights or a simple press
- Ventilation and gloves if the cement is solvent-borne
- A spare swatch for stitch, fuse, cement, and artwork rub tests

## Sequence

### A — Verify

1. Order the ripstop swatch before the yard.
2. Compare the swatch to the reference card in daylight and under 5000 K. Record whether the grid is present.
3. Choose the last and the sole together. The sole needs a wide stance, a raised sidewall, a round toe bumper, and a lug field in the plus character of the card. If no such sole exists in size, stop and record the closest candidate. Do not redesign the upper to suit a different silhouette.
4. Photograph the sole from the side, the front, the rear, and the ground face next to the card.

### B — Trace

1. Cover the last with tape.
2. Mark the feather where the upper should disappear into the sidewall, using the card’s wave as the picture and the real sole as the limit.
3. Mark the collar opening. It is low and generous. The heel stays open.
4. Mark where the logo tab sits: centered on the instep collar, just forward of the heel opening, stitched through the border.
5. Rebuild the upper net from that trace. Retire the DXF files in `patterns/` once the trace exists. Those files are proportion nets with an 8-unit mock-up allowance that is an assumption, not a specification.

### C — Mock-up

1. Cut the shell in paper or inexpensive fabric with the mock-up allowance.
2. Fit it on the last inside the sole cavity if the sole is in hand.
3. Check, against the card: round toe behind the bumper, low vamp, open heel, collar bulk, tab position.
4. Change the paper. Do not “correct” the card.

### D — Upper

1. Cut P01 shell, P02 lining, P03 collar foam, P05 logo tab, P06 toe puff from the traced patterns. Quantities are one shell, one lining, one foam, one tab, and one puff per shoe.
2. If a stabilizer is used, baste or sew it. Fuse only after the swatch still peels clean and the DWR face is not spoiled.
3. Set the toe puff behind the round toe so the bumper, not the puff, makes the outside shape.
4. Sew shell to lining where the opening will roll. Sandwich the foam in the collar. Roll the edge so the outside cloth is the ripstop. Stitch through the roll.
5. A short back seam is allowed only if the trace needs it to close the collar. Keep it inside the collar roll at the back of the opening.
6. Stitch the logo tab on the outside of the instep collar, centered. Border stitch as on the card. Deboss or stamp MADE / SAiNT from the founder file, lowercase i. Do not stitch through the letters.
7. Mount the upper on the last. Leave it until the cloth relaxes. Photograph it beside the card before any cement.

### E — Footbed

1. Fit the cork-latex base in the real cavity. Trim until it sits flat and can be lifted out.
2. Laminate the powder-blue microfiber with the adhesive meant for that skin.
3. Place the supplied Saint Michael file. Head and wings toward the toe. Figure underfoot toward the heel. MADE SAiNT beneath the illustration, toward the heel, lowercase i.
4. Rub a spare print with a dry cloth and a damp cloth. If it smears, change the method before the pair is walked.
5. The drawing on sheet 04 is an orientation window. It is not artwork.

### F — Bottoming

1. Read the cement container. Solvent cement needs moving air and skin protection.
2. Rough only the surfaces the cement maker names. Keep cement off the ripstop that shows above the sidewall.
3. Prime the rubber only if that compound requires it.
4. Cement both faces, dry to the tack the instructions describe, and set the lasted upper into the sole. Press the toe bumper and the heel.
5. Wipe squeeze-out while it is wet.
6. Do not add a separate rand if the sole already has the raised wall.
7. Leave the last in for the cure time on the container. Then remove it and insert the footbed.

### G — Check before anyone calls the pair approved

The memorandum’s acceptance list:

1. Comfortable forefoot and toe clearance on a 12 2E foot.
2. Heel stays in the opening while walking indoors.
3. Sole sits level.
4. No early separation of upper and sole.
5. Collar and lining do not abrade in the first wear.
6. Footbed art is still readable after the rub test.
7. Proportions still match the reference card.

Write the failures on a revision 002 note. Do not silently add elastic, a counter, or a new lug pattern.

## Quality notes for the drawings

- Sheet 01 views describe one shoe. Lateral and medial are the same silhouette because the card does not show a confirmed asymmetry.
- The plus lugs are the observed family. Their spacing on the drawing is a schematic so a buyer can compare a sole. It is not a mold drawing.
- No dimension on the sheets is taken from a last or a sole. Scale is NTS.
- Patterns and DXF files carry the same limit in the file note.
